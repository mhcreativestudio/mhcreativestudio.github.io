Website Experience & Conversion Audit

A structured review of how clearly a website explains its offer, helps visitors evaluate their options, builds confidence and guides them towards an enquiry.

Anonymized work samplePractical placement projectAudit & recommendations

Project overview

Turning website observations into practical recommendations.

The project reviewed a product-led website with a catalogue and enquiry-based buying journey. The aim was to identify where visitors could experience uncertainty, difficulty comparing options or reduced confidence before making contact.

What was reviewed

Website structure, key page messaging, product information, pricing guidance, portfolio presentation, customer proof, policies, mobile usability and the enquiry journey.

My contribution

Website analysis, customer-journey assessment, usability observations, competitor research, issue prioritisation and recommendations.

What this page demonstrates

The reasoning behind selected audit findings—not a claim that the recommendations were implemented or that business results improved.

The audit · Six priority findings

What was getting in the visitor’s way?

Each finding follows the same process: what the review identified, why it could matter to a prospective customer, and what the audit recommended.

01
High priority · Message consistency

Different pages communicated different promises.

What the audit identified

The homepage and enquiry page presented different turnaround promises for the same design-preview offer. Business-count figures also varied between pages.

Why it matters

When important claims change from page to page, visitors may question which information is accurate—especially when they are considering a high-value purchase.

Illustrative page-message comparison
Page A · Main pageDesign preview in one timeframe
Page B · Enquiry pageA different timeframe
Recommended improvement

Verify each promotional claim and use one achievable, consistent promise and one approved set of business figures across all relevant pages.

02
Medium priority · Product education

Visitors were shown options, but not enough help choosing between them.

What the audit identified

The catalogue presented multiple product types, but a first-time buyer could struggle to understand the differences, typical uses, materials, lighting or suitability.

Why it matters

A visitor may know the outcome they want but not the technical option that will achieve it. Too much independent interpretation can delay or prevent an enquiry.

Illustrative comparison module
Recommended improvement

Add a simple comparison guide covering appearance, materials, features, typical applications, indoor/outdoor suitability and relative cost. Consider a guided selector based on the visitor’s needs.

03
Medium priority · Price transparency

Visitors had limited context for estimating the likely investment.

What the audit identified

The reviewed homepage content did not make example price ranges or starting-price guidance easy to find. The quote-based model itself was understandable, but offered little early budget context.

Why it matters

When customers cannot judge whether an option is within budget, some may leave before starting a conversation—even if they are interested.

Illustrative pricing guidance layout
Option detailsSize · materials · features
Typical price rangeExample guide, not a quote
Request an estimateBased on project requirements
Recommended improvement

Where commercially appropriate, provide verified starting prices or clearly labelled example project ranges, with the dimensions and specifications that affect cost. Do not publish invented prices.

04
High priority · Portfolio experience & proof

The portfolio was difficult to explore and did not consistently establish project authenticity.

What the audit identified

The gallery relied on an embedded document viewer, making project details harder to browse—particularly on mobile. The review also found that some polished visuals lacked enough context to distinguish completed work from concepts or mock-ups.

Why it matters

Visitors use project examples to judge quality and capability. Small images, awkward browsing and unclear project provenance weaken that evidence.

Recommended improvement

Replace the embedded document with a responsive project gallery. Use genuine completed-work photographs, concise project details and clear labels for any concept, render or mock-up. Add before-and-after evidence only where authentic images are available.

05
High priority · Review credibility

Some review text and accompanying project images did not clearly match.

What the audit identified

Some customer reviews were paired with photographs that did not appear to support the project described. Other testimonial-style statements lacked visible evidence to verify the customer experience.

Why it matters

When a review and its image tell different stories, visitors may question whether the feedback is genuine or relevant.

06
High priority · Policy clarity & enquiry journey

Important customer expectations were not consistently explained.

What the audit identified

The shipping information and terms page gave different deadlines for reporting damaged items. The audit also identified opportunities to simplify the quote process and improve mobile usability, including the presentation of product information and forms.

Why it matters

Unclear after-sales terms create uncertainty about what happens if something goes wrong. A difficult mobile enquiry process can add friction at the moment a visitor is ready to act.

Illustrative consistency and enquiry checklist

Terms page BA different reporting timeframe

Recommended improvement

Reconcile shipping, damage, warranty, replacement and refund terms. Make the enquiry form easier to complete on mobile, clarify what happens after submission, and track form starts, submitted enquiries and qualified leads.

Audit deliverable

A prioritised improvement roadmap—not an unsupported results claim.

The work produced a structured set of observations and recommendations across messaging, buying guidance, pricing context, portfolio presentation, customer proof, policies and enquiry measurement. The next step for the business would be to validate priorities, implement agreed changes and measure performance against a baseline.

What this work demonstrates

Analysis before recommendation.

A useful audit should connect the visible website issue to a real customer question, explain the potential impact and recommend an action that can be checked.

Measurement considerations

Track meaningful actions.

  • Landing-page and product-page views
  • Enquiry-form starts and submissions
  • Qualified enquiries and cost per qualified lead
  • Response time and progression to purchase
  • Purchase conversion, where reliable data is available
